Why the line turns south, not north

An earlier version looped north out of Karaman onto the Konya steppe. It made the distance, but it climbed 1,001 metres in 141 kilometres and sat between 1,013 and 1,020 metres the whole way. The manual asks for 9,950 metres on this leg, which rules that ground out.

The line now runs south over the Sertavul pass at about 1,650 metres, through Tarlaören, Tavşanlı, Ağaçyurdu and Medreselik. Same distance, 1,557 metres more climbing, and it found the km 542 village.

A correction to the method

He is known to use goat tracks and unmapped surfaces. An unmapped track can beat the mapped road network, so shortest mapped road is not a disproof — it is only a warning. A track still cannot beat a straight line.

Re-testing on that basis: İbradı and Ormana stay dead, because they need a route shorter than a straight line. Derebucak, Ermenek, Sarıveliler and Hadim all come back, but at route factors of 1.08 to 1.33. Leg 1 runs at 1.88 end to end, so the straightest of those revivals are still weak. Ermenek at 1.33 is the one that genuinely returns.

What the missing ascent costs

6,180 metres is worth about 8 hours of moving time and, at a 29 per cent stopped share, about 11 hours elapsed. That is close to half a day, and it is the difference between a 7.6 day race and an 8.0 day race on the field model.

Why no ascent figure is quoted

The same line measures anywhere between 8,800 and 12,900 metres depending only on how densely the elevation is sampled.

The manual’s 8,903 m sits inside that range. RideWithGPS smooths heavily and reads at the low end; raw sampling inflates. Never compare two ascent figures unless they were sampled the same way. Resample both to 200 metres first.

Three ideas that did not survive the ground

An Aladağlar loop, a direct crossing from Karagöl to photograph D, and a contour held from km 188 to 212. All three were proposed from arithmetic and all three were wrong. The Karagöl line crosses an unrideable face — eighteen of twenty-four sampled segments steeper than 25 per cent, with a 66 per cent climb and a 72 per cent drop. The contour has no shelf to sit on; the valley at km 190 is a confluence and the only way out is up the drainage.

Photograph D is 3 km off the line and should stay that way. It is a viewpoint on the south side of the crest, not a waypoint. It still proves the range is crossed; it does not prove the route touches that spot.

The Bolkar evidence

All four scouting coordinates sit on one line through the Bolkar Dağları. One of them lands 200 metres from Karagöl lake. Another sits 4.6 kilometres south-west of Medetsiz, the 3,524 metre high point — that is on the far side of the crest, which means the route crosses the range from north to south.

A north-to-south crossing only makes sense after Checkpoint 3. It fits the 116 kilometre gap between resupply 23 (km 1,176) and resupply 24 (km 1,292).
